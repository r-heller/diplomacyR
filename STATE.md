## Rule interpretations

Where the rulebooks admit more than one reading, the choice below follows the
preference recorded in the DATC.

* **Convoying to an adjacent province.** Intent decides. An army with both a
  land route and an ordered convoy route travels by convoy only when the order
  says "via convoy" or when one of the army's own fleets sits on a
  geographically possible convoy route. A foreign convoy order cannot
  commandeer the army (DATC 6.G.2-6.G.4), and a friendly fleet nowhere near a
  possible route does not show intent (6.G.7).
* **Convoy paradoxes.** Broken by the Szykman rule: the convoys inside the
  cycle are disrupted. A cycle of pure moves is circular movement and every
  mover advances.
* **An army ordered to a province it cannot reach overland.** Treated as an
  attempted convoy -- so it cannot receive support to hold -- whenever fleets
  on the board make a convoy conceivable, and as an impossible order otherwise
  (DATC 6.D.8 versus 6.D.32).
* **Unspecified coasts.** A fleet ordered to a split-coast province without
  naming a coast moves when exactly one coast is reachable and holds when two
  are (DATC 6.B.1-6.B.3). Support is given at province level, but a support
  that names a coast must name the one the move used (6.B.9).
* **Civil-disorder disbands.** The unit furthest from home goes first, with
  distance measured over regular moves for fleets and over the combined land
  and sea graph for armies; a fleet goes before an army at equal distance, and
  ties break alphabetically by province (DATC 4.D.8, choice d).

## Nothing outstanding

The `negotiator` bot ships as documented but is deliberately shallow: the
package has no negotiation protocol, so the bot substitutes a board-leader
threat model. It is marked experimental in its own documentation.
